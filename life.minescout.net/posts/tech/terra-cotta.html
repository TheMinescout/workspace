The AI One-Shot Battle: Creating the Terra Cotta Game Suite

Posted on 2/13/26 by TheMinescouter

I challenged the world's top AI models to a "One-Shot" coding battle. The rules were simple but brutal: create a complete, single-file HTML5 game suite with five specific arcade games (Tic-Tac-Toe, Snake, Minesweeper, Dino Run, Pac-Man) and a multiplayer backend using PeerJS. They got one prompt and one try. No human bug fixes allowed.

The results were surprising. Here is the breakdown of the prompt they faced and how each model performed under the pressure.

The Prompt

"Code a complete, single-file HTML5 game application called 'Terra Cotta Game Suite' that requires no external assets... Implement the following five games within the suite: Tic-Tac-Toe Suite (Normal, 3D, Ultimate)... Snake... Minesweeper... Dino Run... Pac-Man Lite... Ensure all game logic is modular and that the 3D Tic-Tac-Toe board includes a visual stack animation when a player wins."

The Contenders

Google Gemini

Model: Gemini 3 Pro

Play Gemini's Output »
Minescout's Review:
Gemini delivered a great first draft. It clearly understood the architecture required for a single-file game hub. However, there were a couple of glaring issues with how it handled the win logic and the requested CSS animations. It also left behind a few small, annoying bugs that kept it from being a perfect run.

ChatGPT

Model: o3-mini

Play ChatGPT's Output »
Minescout's Review:
ChatGPT successfully crammed all the requested games into the file, but the quality varied wildly. Snake played great, but Tic-Tac-Toe was absolutely awful. Its version of "Pac-Man" was basically just moving a generic sphere around a screen. Dino Run was fun, but it forgot to include a "lose" condition, making you immortal. The biggest failure? The PeerJS multiplayer was completely broken and not really an option.
emoji_events WINNER

Claude

Model: Claude 3.7 Sonnet

Play Claude's Output »
Minescout's Review:
Claude's output was visually stunning. It absolutely nailed the "Terra Cotta" Material Design aesthetic I asked for. Almost all of the arcade games worked flawlessly right out of the gate. Its only real downfalls were a broken win animation and a completely busted 3D Tic-Tac-Toe mode.

The Verdict

The overall winner is Claude.

Despite dropping the ball on the 3D Tic-Tac-Toe logic, Claude's incredible grasp of the design system, UI scaling, and the fact that the majority of the arcade games were highly playable on the first attempt gave it the clear edge over Gemini and ChatGPT.
